Correction — the carbon tax is not a price ceiling

Correcting Session 05, §6 and Fig. 5.3

I said the carbon tax rate caps Indonesia's allowance price. The statute says the opposite.

In Session 05 I reasoned that because Perpres 110 Pasal 63(3) makes exceeding your cap trigger an obligation to pay carbon tax, no rational firm would ever pay more than the tax rate for a quota — so the tax rate is an effective price ceiling. That is the standard hybrid design, and I applied it without reading UU HPP Pasal 13. I should have read it first.

The Directorate General of Taxes states the rule: the carbon tax rate is set equal to or higher than the carbon market price per kilogram CO₂e, and where the market price falls below Rp30,00 per kg CO₂e, the rate is set at a minimum of Rp30,00.2

What I said

Tax rate is fixed → paying tax is cheaper than a quota above that level → the tax caps the market price.

What the law provides

Tax rate tracks the market upward and never sits below it → paying tax is never cheaper than complying → the tax is a floor under compliance, not a ceiling on price.

Why it matters, and why the error was substantive rather than cosmetic: a price ceiling weakens a cap — it caps the abatement signal. A rate pegged at or above the market does the reverse: it removes the option of buying your way out cheaply, so the cap can bind as hard as the government sets it. Indonesia's design is stronger than I described, not weaker.

The Rp30,00/kg floor is Rp30,000 per tonne CO₂e — low by international standards [?] (I am not converting to dollars; the rate depends on an exchange rate I have not fixed to a source). But it is a floor, and it only binds when the market is weaker still.

The lesson to take from this, not just the fix

I reached the wrong answer by reasoning correctly from a generic model of hybrid instruments instead of reading the specific statute. That is exactly the failure mode this curriculum's sourcing discipline exists to catch — and it is the failure mode you should expect from any adviser, consultant or briefing note that describes Indonesia's system by analogy to the EU's.

Session 05's own source note flagged UU HPP as “not opened; the rate is an explicit reading item for Session 08.” The flag was right; the surrounding analysis went further than the flag permitted. When a source is flagged unread, the conclusions built on it should be flagged too. I will hold that line more carefully.

Perpres 110 was promulgated 10 October 2025. The Second NDC was submitted 27 October 2025 — seventeen days later.

That sequencing is not coincidence. Session 07 found the NDC citing Perpres 110 by name as “a legal framework to implement NDC.” Indonesia put the domestic legal machinery in place first, then submitted an international pledge that points at it. Read together, the two documents are a single act: the pledge and the apparatus to deliver it, filed a fortnight apart.

2

The number cascade

Chapter II is one long derivation. Every number in the system descends from Alokasi Karbon, and each step names who decides.

Fig. 8.1 — The derivation chain, with articles and page numbers as printed.1

Two features are worth naming. First, Pasal 22(2) requires every mitigation target — national, sector and provincial — to be expressed as GHG emission reduction in tonnes CO₂e. The regulation forces an absolute-tonnage statement at every level of government, which is exactly the translation problem Session 01 predicted if installation-level obligations turn out to be intensity-based.

Second, Pasal 23(3) makes the relationship with Session 07 explicit rather than inferential: the national mitigation target, once set by the Minister, is “dituangkan dalam dokumen NDC” — poured into the NDC document. The NDC is the output of this cascade, not an external constraint on it.

The baseline can move — and the conditions are enumerated

Pasal 18 (pp.18–19) permits the national or sector baseline to change on four grounds: a change in national development policy relating to climate; the addition of new Data Aktivitas; a change in Faktor Emisi; or a methodological change materially affecting the calculation. The same four grounds appear for Alokasi Karbon at Pasal 5 (p.9) and, with provincial variants, at Pasal 21 (p.21).

Why a developer should care: your project's creditable volume is measured against a baseline that the state may lawfully revise mid-stream, through a defined procedure, on grounds including “better data”. Combined with the Article 6.4 renewal reassessment from Session 04 (¶57), that is two independent mechanisms by which a credit stream can legitimately shrink without anything going wrong at the project.

Three consequences for an energy practitioner

1 — ESDM is your counterparty for four sub-sectors, not one. Power generation, oil and gas, transport and buildings all sit under the same coordinator. If you work across generation and industrial energy use, note the split: industrial process and energy use falls to Perindustrian, not ESDM.

2 — The offset limit is set by the sectoral minister, not the environment ministry. Session 03 located that dial at Pasal 62(1)(d); Pasal 62(1) assigns the whole ETS operation to Menteri Terkait. So how much offsetting your sector may use will be decided by ESDM or Perindustrian — which is where a developer's engagement should be directed.

3 — Forestry and environment are now different ministries. Session 07 found the UNFCCC focal point moved to the Ministry of Environment; here, Menteri Kehutanan appears as a distinct sector coordinator and as a distinct member of the steering committee. Anyone still writing “KLHK” is describing a body that has been split.

Additionality — the finding is definitive, and negative

Session 00 flagged it. Session 03 could not find it. Having now read the operative regulation end to end, I can state it plainly.

Adisionalitas appears nowhere in Perpres 110/2025

Not in the 44 definitions of Pasal 1 (pp.2–7). Not in the mitigation planning chapter, Pasal 8–34 (pp.11–31). Not in the NEK implementation chapter, Pasal 55–74 (pp.42–51). Not in the validation and verification provisions, Pasal 83–86 (pp.56–58), nor in SPE-GRK issuance at Pasal 88–89 (pp.59–60).

The regulation reaches the function only obliquely: Pasal 65(3)(c), p.46 requires a DRAM or DPP to state its “application of national, UNFCCC, and other international methodology and standards.” Additionality therefore enters Indonesian practice through whichever methodology a project adopts — which for an international-standard route means the Article 6.4 test at ¶38, read in Session 04, or a voluntary standard's equivalent.

Where it will be written down domestically is signposted twice: Pasal 34, p.31 sends the detailed mitigation procedures to a Peraturan Menteri, and Pasal 102, p.67 requires all implementing regulations to be established within one year.

The single most consequential test for whether a project earns credits is not in the Presidential Regulation. It is in ministerial rules that were not yet published when this regulation took effect.

That is not unusual drafting — a Perpres sets architecture. But it does mean that anyone telling you today what qualifies as additional in Indonesia is either citing a ministerial regulation you should ask to see, or is extrapolating. Ask which.

Pasal 99 is the one to notice. Any bilateral carbon arrangement Indonesia had agreed before 10 October 2025 — the kind of cooperation Session 03 discussed under Article 6.2 — is required to be brought into conformity with this regulation, including its Otorisasi and corresponding-adjustment architecture. [?] Which specific agreements that captures, and what conformity has been achieved, is not something this regulation tells you and I have not verified it.

The closing provisions do something subtle

Pasal 101, p.67: Perpres 98/2021 is “dicabut dan dinyatakan tidak berlaku” — revoked and declared no longer in force.

Pasal 100, p.67: but the implementing regulations of Perpres 98/2021 — together with those of Perpres 61/2011 (RAN-GRK) and Perpres 71/2011 (national GHG inventory) — “dinyatakan tetap berlaku”, remain in force, insofar as they do not conflict with this Perpres or have not yet been replaced under it.

So the parent regulation is dead and its children live on. Practically: the ministerial regulations you would consult today were mostly written under a regulation that no longer exists, and they remain valid only to the extent they do not contradict Perpres 110. Reading any Permen from the 98/2021 era therefore requires checking it clause by clause against the new architecture — which is precisely the kind of work that produces confident-sounding but wrong advice.

The clock — and where we are on it

Fig. 8.2 — Pasal 102, p.67: implementing regulations “ditetapkan paling lambat 1 (satu) tahun sejak Peraturan Presiden ini diundangkan.” Promulgation date from p.68.1

Everything converges on 10 October 2026: the implementing regulations that will contain additionality, methodology, MRV procedure and the sectoral offset limits; the deadline for existing unit-holders to come into compliance; the deadline for adjusting pre-existing international agreements; and the deadline for realigning national and regional action plans.

This is the most decision-relevant fact in the session. The rules that determine whether an Indonesian project is creditable, and on what terms, are due within weeks of now. 6

Three lenses

Government

The cascade concentrates real discretion in two places: the joint ministerial decision on Alokasi Karbon, and each sectoral minister's offset limit and cap. The steering committee under a Coordinating Minister exists to hold those together. Note also that Pasal 22(2) forces tonnage statements at every level — there is no hiding an intensity target inside the cascade.

Project developer

Pasal 98 may already bind you: if you hold Unit Karbon and have not transacted, you had one year from 10 October 2025 to conform. And your creditability rests on ministerial rules due 10 October 2026 — so the honest position today is that the test you must pass is not yet fully published.

Project / asset owner

Two revision risks to price, not one: the baseline may be lawfully revised under Pasal 18, and an Article 6.4 crediting period is reassessed at renewal (¶57). Add the Pasal 100 problem — advice resting on Perpres 98-era ministerial regulations may be citing rules that survive only conditionally.

Comprehension check

  1. Why does a carbon tax rate pegged at or above the market price strengthen a cap-and-trade system, where a fixed rate would weaken it?
  2. Trace the derivation from Alokasi Karbon to the cap on a single power plant. Name each intermediate number and who sets it.
  3. Perpres 98/2021 is revoked but its implementing regulations remain in force. What practical problem does that create for anyone advising on Indonesian carbon rules today?
  4. Your client is an industrial energy-efficiency developer. Which ministry sets the offset limit that determines their compliance-market demand — and why is it not the environment ministry?
  5. Additionality is absent from Perpres 110. Where does it enter Indonesian practice in the meantime, and what should you ask anyone who states an Indonesian additionality rule today?
