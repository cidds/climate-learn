The two decisions that most determine a project's economics belong to different ministries.

Whether your credit can be exported at the higher authorised price is the environment minister's call under Pasal 68(2). Whether there is any domestic compliance demand for it at all — the offset limit under Pasal 62(1)(d) — is the sectoral minister's. A developer who engages only one has engaged half the system.

Why a Coordinating Minister for Food chairs it

The steering committee is chaired by the Menteri Koordinator Bidang Pangan, with the Coordinating Ministers for Economic Affairs and for Infrastructure as deputies (Pasal 96(4), pp.64–65). Placing carbon governance under the food portfolio rather than the economic one is a structural choice worth registering: it groups carbon with land, forestry and agriculture rather than with markets and finance.

Two members are easy to overlook and both are telling. Ketua Dewan Komisioner OJK sits on the committee — with Pasal 96(5) expressly preserving OJK's independence — which acknowledges that carbon units are financial instruments. And Kepala BMKG, the meteorology, climatology and geophysics agency, brings the physical-science function into a body otherwise composed of ministries.

The division is the same one Session 00 drew in Fig. 0.1: what you did versus what you hold. An action can exist in SRN PPI without ever producing a tradable unit — a results-based payment project, for instance, which under Pasal 70(3) transfers no ownership at all. A unit cannot exist without an action behind it.

Verification — and the two accreditation routes

Resolved in Session 03 from Pasal 85. Restated here because it is the cleanest illustration of how the whole system bifurcates.

One function, two accreditors — split by which market you are in

An LVV must be a legal entity, hold sector-competent validators and verifiers, and be accredited by either:1

KAN — Komite Akreditasi Nasional — for the SRUK carbon-unit issuance scheme;
and/or an internationally valid accreditation body, for units issued under an international carbon registration scheme.

Validator and verifier competence standards are set by a KAN regulation (Pasal 85(2)); procedures by a Peraturan Menteri Terkait (Pasal 86).

Notice the pattern repeating down the stack. DRAM → domestic → KAN. DPP → international standard → international accreditation. The fork chosen at project design in Session 00 propagates all the way to who is allowed to audit you.

Carbon units are legally securities in Indonesia. That is a bigger fact than it sounds.

It brings the entire capital-markets apparatus with it — licensing, market conduct supervision, settlement, governance, risk management and consumer protection all sit with OJK. It also means a carbon transaction is a securities transaction, with the disclosure and intermediation obligations that implies. Anyone modelling an Indonesian credit as a simple commodity offtake is missing a regulatory layer.

A timing problem worth watching — POJK 14/2023 predates Perpres 110

POJK 14/2023 was issued under the Perpres 98/2021 regime and refers to registration in SRN-PPI. Perpres 110/2025 revoked Perpres 98 (Pasal 101) and routes unit recording to SRUK (Pasal 59(1)), a registry SRN-PPI's own definition does not cover.

Under Pasal 100 the older implementing rules remain in force insofar as they do not conflict and have not been replaced. So POJK 14/2023 is presumably still operative — but its registry reference now points at an architecture the parent regulation has restructured. This is exactly the Session 08 problem, in the wild: a live financial-markets regulation whose plumbing references have moved beneath it. Whether OJK has updated it is [?] — I have not verified, and it is worth checking before relying on the registration pathway.

Note the asymmetry that Session 06 identified, now visible in the institutional design: results-based payment brings money in without giving the tonne away, while trading brings money in and — where authorised — surrenders it. The same ministry does not control both channels, which is why the steering committee has a dedicated fiscal field chaired by the finance minister.

The frame Indonesia uses for all of this

Worth knowing, because it explains design choices that otherwise look merely bureaucratic.

The Ministry of Forestry presents Perpres 110 as strengthening state sovereignty over carbon, grounding it in Article 33 of the 1945 Constitution — natural resources are controlled by the state for the greatest prosperity of the people — and stating that carbon economic value management sits under the full control of the Government of Indonesia. The stated ambition is Indonesian carbon credits that are world-class, high-integrity and globally recognised, without setting aside national development priorities.3

Read the authorisation architecture as sovereignty, not bureaucracy, and it stops looking like friction.

Every gate this curriculum has catalogued — Otorisasi at Pasal 68(2), Menteri Terkait approval even on the international-standard route at Pasal 65(6), mandatory SRUK recording at Pasal 59(1) — is an expression of the same constitutional claim: carbon is a national resource, and the state decides the terms on which it leaves. A developer who reads these as permitting hurdles will keep being surprised. A developer who reads them as a sovereign asserting control over an export will anticipate them.

[?] This is a government framing of its own regulation, sourced to a ministry communication rather than to the Perpres text. The regulation's own recitals, read in Session 00, cite climate and sustainable development grounds rather than Article 33 — so treat the sovereignty framing as authoritative about intent, not as operative law.

What is still missing

An honest inventory, one month before the Pasal 102 deadline.

I searched for implementing regulations under Perpres 110 and did not find them

Session 08 established that all implementing regulations are due by 10 October 2026 (Pasal 102), and that additionality, MRV procedure, methodology detail and the sectoral offset limits all live in that layer. A search this session returned the Perpres itself, government commentary and legal-practice notes — but no ministerial regulation issued under it.

That is not proof of absence. Indonesian ministerial regulations are often published on ministry JDIH portals that a general search reaches poorly, and my search was in one language against one index. What it does mean is that I cannot tell you these rules exist, and neither should anyone who has not shown you the document.

Concretely, these remain unanswerable from what I hold: what additionality test applies; what the sectoral offset limits are; which installations are Instalasi yang Diatur; what the Batas Atas Emisi is for power or industry; whether POJK 14/2023 has been aligned to SRUK; and whether pre-existing international agreements have been adjusted under Pasal 99.

8

Three lenses

Government

The split of powers is deliberate and it has a cost: the ministry that issues credits does not set the demand for them, and the body that supervises the market answers to neither. The steering committee is the only place those threads meet — which makes its secretariat, and the Menko Pangan regulation defining it (Pasal 96(8)), unusually consequential for a piece of machinery nobody watches.

Project developer

Map your approvals to institutions, not to a process. You need the sectoral ministry for recording, recommendation and demand; the Ministry of Environment for issuance and authorisation; a KAN- or internationally accredited LVV depending on your route; SRUK registration; and if you intend to trade on exchange, OJK-regulated intermediation for what is legally a security.

Project / asset owner

Two diligence points specific to this session. First, an Indonesian carbon unit traded on exchange is a security — check the intermediation and disclosure chain, not just the offtake. Second, the offset limit that creates your domestic demand has probably not been set yet; if a model shows compliance-market revenue, ask which ministerial regulation it relies on.

Comprehension check

  1. Name the two ministries whose separate decisions determine, respectively, whether your credit can be exported and whether anyone in Indonesia must buy it.
  2. An action is recorded in SRN PPI but no unit ever appears in SRUK. Give a lawful scenario where that is exactly what should happen.
  3. Why does the LVV accreditation rule have two limbs, and how does it mirror a choice made much earlier in a project's life?
  4. Carbon units traded on IDXCarbon are legally securities. Name two consequences that follow for a buyer which would not follow if they were commodities.
  5. POJK 14/2023 requires registration in SRN-PPI; Perpres 110 routes unit recording to SRUK. Explain the legal position under Pasal 100, and what you would check before relying on either.
