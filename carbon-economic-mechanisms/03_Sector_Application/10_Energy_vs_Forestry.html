Flag closed — Indonesia's power ETS is intensity-benchmarked

Session 01 said this [?] and refused to assert it. Sessions 05 and 08 kept it open. ESDM's own account settles it, with numbers.

Permen ESDM No. 16 Tahun 2022 — the power sub-sector NEK rules

PTBAE — Persetujuan Teknis Batas Atas Emisi — is the technical approval setting the highest permitted GHG emission level for power plants in a period. PTBAE-PU is the emission quota allocated to an individual business actor, expressed in tonnes CO₂e.2

Crucially, ESDM states the caps are set as intensity benchmarks per MWh, not absolute tonnage. Illustrative Phase I coal values it gives:

Phase I coal categoryBenchmark
Smaller facilities1.297 tCO₂e/MWh
Larger non-mine-mouth plants above 400 MW0.911 tCO₂e/MWh
Spreadthe large-plant benchmark is 29.8% tighter

Coverage: Phase I ran 2023–2024, applying to coal plants ≥100 MW connected to the PLN grid — roughly 99 units, in four categories differentiated by size and location. Three further phases extend the scheme through 2030. [?] The full benchmark table, the category definitions and the later phases' scope are in the regulation itself, which I have not opened — these are ESDM's illustrative figures. The regulation is also dated inconsistently across sources (enacted late December 2022; ESDM announced it 24 January 2023), so cite the regulation, not a date, until you have the text.

Session 01 predicted a translation problem. It exists, and it is now specified: intensity at the installation, absolute tonnage at the nation.

Perpres 110 Pasal 22(2) requires every mitigation target — national, sector, provincial — to be stated in tonnes CO₂e, and Alokasi Karbon is defined as an absolute CO₂e quantity. But the instrument that actually binds a power plant allocates by tCO₂e per MWh. Those two are only reconcilable through an assumption about how much electricity gets generated.

Why the cap grows when the plant does

This is Session 05's output-based allocation, in Indonesian practice. The arithmetic makes it concrete.

Why a government would choose this anyway — and it is not simply weakness

Session 01 set out the case: an intensity target is politically survivable in a growing economy because it does not cap growth. Fig. 10.1 sharpens why that matters here. A system that is 67.5% coal and where wind and solar are 0.26% cannot absorb an absolute cap without either curtailing electricity supply or importing abatement it cannot yet build.

Benchmarking also does real work that a flat cap would not: the 29.8% spread between the two Phase I benchmarks means efficient large plants receive surplus quota to sell and inefficient plants must buy. Within the coal fleet, that is a genuine merit-order pressure. What it cannot do is reduce the fleet.

The honest formulation: Indonesia has built an instrument that improves the efficiency of a growing coal fleet, not one that shrinks it. Whether that is the right first step or an insufficient one is a judgement — but it should be made knowing which of the two the instrument is.

3

Industrial energy efficiency — the squeeze

Your third energy sub-sector, and the one where the mechanisms interact most awkwardly.

Three constraints closing in on the same projects

1 — The regulatory-surplus test. Decision -/CMA.3 ¶38 requires creditable mitigation to exceed what law or regulation already requires. PP No. 33/2023 mandates energy management above a threshold of energy use, minimum energy performance standards and efficiency labelling across industry, buildings and transport.3 Every tightening of MEPS moves efficiency measures from creditable to merely compliant.

2 — Negative-cost measures need more than a price. Session 01's Fig. 1.1 showed motor, pump and building measures sitting below zero cost and still undone — because the barriers are split incentives, capital rationing and information, not price. A carbon price does not unlock them; obligation or finance does.

3 — Industry sits with a different ministry. Under Pasal 24(2)(b) the industry sub-sector is coordinated by Menteri Perindustrian, not ESDM. So the offset limit that would create compliance demand for industrial efficiency credits is set by a ministry separate from the one running the power scheme.

The cheapest abatement in your sector is the hardest to monetise through carbon markets.

Industrial energy efficiency is where the negative-cost tonnes are — and it is simultaneously the most exposed to the additionality squeeze, the least responsive to a price signal, and administratively split from the power scheme. Expect the compliance market to deliver less efficiency abatement than the MACC suggests is available, and treat efficiency policy as a regulatory and finance problem with a carbon-market component, rather than the reverse.

Note what the 2nd FRL did: it more than halved the reference level, from 0.568 to 0.264 GtCO₂e per year.

A forest reference level is a baseline — Session 00's most consequential number, at national scale. Halving it means the same physical performance now generates far fewer creditable reductions against it. Indonesia tightened its own forest baseline, which is the opposite of the incentive Session 06 found operating in project-level REDD+.

Why forestry sits in VCM and results-based payment rather than the ETS

Session 01 gave the structural reasons and every one of them now has an Indonesian citation. Forestry produces removals with reversal risk — and Indonesia's own emissions history proves the point: the NDC reports 2019 emissions dominated by LUCF including peat fires at 50.13%, against 2022 emissions dominated by energy at 69.01%. The difference is largely fire. A cap-and-trade obligation cannot sensibly be placed on an asset whose emissions swing that hard on weather.

Counterparties are dispersed, with land-tenure and community-rights questions an allowance market has no way to handle. And Pasal 70(3) gives the decisive institutional answer: results-based payment transfers no carbon ownership, and under Pasal 70(4) the reductions stay in Indonesia's NDC. For a sector that must deliver a 389,796 Gg swing, that is exactly the instrument you want — payment without surrender.

[?] Whether forestry in fact dominates Indonesian voluntary-market issuance — flagged in Session 06 — remains unverified; I have not pulled registry data. The structural argument stands regardless of the issuance split.

The sector with the most carbon to sell is the one Indonesia can least afford to sell from.

Forestry generates the removals international buyers want and holds the credits with the strongest voluntary-market demand. It is also the sector delivering the 389,796 Gg swing on which the NDC depends. Every authorised forest tonne exported under Pasal 68 comes out of that swing — while energy, which has abundant creditable activity in principle, is largely inside the compliance system where Session 04's regulatory-surplus test bites.

That tension is the single most important thing to carry into Session 11. It is not a flaw in the design; it is the real constraint the design has to manage.

A denominator trap worth avoiding

PP 40/2025 sets new and renewable energy at 19–23% by 2030 — but of the primary energy supply mix, which includes transport fuel, industrial heat and cooking, not only electricity. Fig. 10.1's 11.5% is electricity generation by PLN.

These are different denominators and the numbers are not comparable. Mixing them is one of the commonest errors in Indonesian energy commentary — including in otherwise careful briefing notes. When someone quotes a renewable share, ask which denominator.

7

Three lenses

Government

You have an instrument that improves coal-fleet efficiency while the fleet grows, and a forest sector carrying the national target that you therefore cannot afford to sell from. The reconciling variable is the benchmark trajectory — how fast tCO₂e/MWh tightens across the phases to 2030 is the single most consequential number in Indonesian energy carbon policy, and it is set by ESDM.

Project developer

In power, your addressable market is quota trading among ~99 covered units, plus whatever offset limit ESDM sets. In industrial efficiency, check MEPS under PP 33/2023 before building an additionality case, and note your ministry is Perindustrian. In forestry, expect authorisation to be hard — those tonnes are load-bearing.

Project / asset owner

An intensity benchmark means a coal asset's carbon liability scales with dispatch, not with a fixed cap — model it per MWh, not as an annual tonnage. And your exposure is to the benchmark trajectory, which is a regulatory variable revised phase by phase, not a market price you can hedge.
